Family-based immigration lets U.S. citizens and lawful permanent residents
(green card holders) petition for certain relatives to become permanent
residents. Eligibility and wait times depend on the petitioner's status and the
relationship.

## Immediate relatives of U.S. citizens

Immediate relatives have an unlimited number of visas available each year, so
there is no wait for a visa to become available. This category includes:

- Spouses of U.S. citizens
- Unmarried children under 21 of U.S. citizens
- Parents of U.S. citizens (the citizen must be 21 or older)

## Family preference categories

For more distant relationships, and for relatives of permanent residents, a
limited number of visas are available each year, which can mean a wait:

- First preference (F1): unmarried sons and daughters (21 or older) of U.S. citizens
- Second preference (F2A/F2B): spouses and children of permanent residents
- Third preference (F3): married sons and daughters of U.S. citizens
- Fourth preference (F4): siblings of U.S. citizens (citizen must be 21 or older)

## The general process

1. The U.S. citizen or permanent resident files **Form I-130**, Petition for
   Alien Relative, to establish the qualifying relationship.
2. Once a visa is available, the relative applies for permanent residence —
   either with **Form I-485** (if in the United States) or through consular
   processing abroad.
